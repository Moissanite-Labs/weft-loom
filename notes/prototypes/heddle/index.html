How to read it

  • Cloth: a network of 467 weights turns each point into a colour. Every input is on screen, so the picture is the model's whole behaviour.
  • Shafts: the directions in weight space that change the picture most per step. To first order, one unit of travel changes the outputs by one unit, whichever shaft you move.
  • Treadles: named changes, each tied to the shafts. The percentage is the share of the named change these shafts can carry.
  • Heddles: points you pin. The loom is threaded around them, so pinned points do not shift to first order.

What a beat checks

  • Weft's own gate: the weights stay within a fixed distance of the baseline and the budget covers the pick.
  • A claim: no point of the cloth changes by more than the limit. A search looks for one point that breaks it, and it alone decides. If it finds one, the pick snaps and the cloth stays.
  • Fresh probes, drawn from the new weights' hash after the pick is fixed, measure what the pick did. They do not decide it.
  • The closer a pick comes to its limits, the longer you must hold to make it.

The record

Every beat, snap and unpick is chained by hash into a draft. Anyone can replay it without this page: python3 replay_draft.py draft.json
