# Where Weft stands: new ground, what others would value, threads worth pulling

15 November 2026. The owner asked where Weft has tread meaningful new ground, what in it would interest others, and which threads might lead there if pulled.

An earlier evaluation the same day (siliconcordat issue 16, recorded in `experiments/weft-threads-2026-11-15`) answered from a reading of two snapshots. This one re-ran the work, added the controls that were missing, read the open pull requests and issues at their heads, and searched the literature for the nearest published work.

Marks: **[F]** re-run or measured for this record, **[R]** read from a record or issue and not re-run, **[K]** published work, **[I]** inference, **[C]** conjecture.

## Short answer

1. [I] **No mechanism in Weft is yet shown to be new.** The earlier evaluation said the same. It now rests on reruns and controls, not on reading.
2. [F] **Three results are solid today and useful beyond the project.** They are the price-display effect in agent colonies, the map of how far a model can move before its behaviour breaks, and the measured error rate of sentences that no program checks.
3. [I] **Two lines could become new ground, and each has one test that would decide it.** They are transplanting a skill between independently trained networks through a small distilled packet, and admitting a self-modification as a behaviour claim that anyone can refute with one input.
4. [F] **One convergence ties six records together.** The same subspace, the directions a set of probes cannot see, is used as the safe channel in one family of records and as the attack channel in another. Both are right, and the measurements say when.

## The three solid results

### Price display moves agents to the cheapest model

[F] In the SP3 block of the colony laws, cells shown a price list chose the cheapest model at their first act in 26 of 32 cases. Cells not shown it stayed on the default in 32 of 32. Every one of the 8 runs with prices beat every one of the 8 without, which has an exact probability of 0.000155 by chance.

[F] Hidden prices cost between 2.0 and 2.9 times the energy under every treatment of truncated runs. The first-choice counts were recounted by two programs written apart.

[K] A short search found routing tools and budget heuristics, and no published controlled experiment in which agents choose their own model from a displayed price list.

[F] What is not shown: the model names "light", "mid" and "heavy" appear in both arms, so reading prices is not yet separated from reading a price block. One model family. The second claimed law, that showing a cell its score raises its reach, is carried by one task and vanishes without it.

### How far a model can move before its behaviour breaks

[F] The calibration study in `experiments/gate-metric-calibration-2026-11-15` was preregistered and run on 12 hosts of two architectures. Its results are in that record's README. In short:

- [F] On the MLP all fourteen predictions were confirmed. On the transformer eight of ten were, and two were not. With 96 probes, the probe check and the first-order distance each missed the stated factor of 1.25 on two or three hosts, for one class of move.
- [F] The worst direction changes outputs about 24 times more than a random one of the same length on the MLP, and about 37 times on the transformer. On the MLP the adversary breaks the retention bar at a distance of 0.1 to 0.3, and one optimiser step is 1.01 long. On neither model is any radius both a behaviour guarantee and wide enough for a single training step.
- [F] The benign graft travels 28 times farther than that step and disturbs native behaviour less than half as much. A permutation of hidden units travels farther still and changes nothing.
- [F] On the MLP, change measured on 256 probes predicts held-out change within 0.93 to 1.08 for every move made without sight of the probes. Against a proposer who knows the probes it is off by a median factor of 23.
- [F] A probe check sees a share of the inputs, not a worst case. On the three transformer hosts that missed, the adversary's move put more than half of its change on one input in twenty.
- [F] The proved bound held on every host and is about five times stricter than a search needs.
- [F] For a single linear map, which is what the Plasm cell is, the gate's bound is exact. A radius of 0.127 keeps the worst case under 5 percent of the cell's output. The shipped 120 allows 47 times the output.

[K] That weight distance and function distance come apart is published (Benjamin, Rolnick and Körding, 2019). What this study adds is the adversarial reading, the proved bound, the comparison with one optimiser step, and the exact rule for a linear cell.

### Sentences that no program checks are wrong four times in ten

[F] In the weft README and Papers 0 to 2, the numbers in 68 sentences are regenerated by CI. None was wrong. Of the other 86 sentences, 35 were wrong or unsupported. Across 93 review findings on records and pull requests, 70 of the 71 with a direction claim more than the evidence. In the software text the direction flips: 20 of 27 claim less than the code now does. `review-meta.md` has the counts.

[I] This is a measurement others can use. It says where an agent-run research programme loses accuracy, and that the loss stops where a program ties the sentence to its file.

## The two candidates for new ground

### A skill moved between independently trained networks through a distilled packet

[F] The E13 splice reproduces exactly. With its admission thresholds frozen, 48 new host and donor pairs in 48 new task worlds gave 47 passes of the joint bar.

[K] The nearest published work found today transplants neurons or modules without training (ARM, January 2026, and Yoon and others, February 2026). From their abstracts both work between related models, where units correspond.

[F] E13 does something different. It never copies a unit. It distils the donor's behaviour on one skill into a packet of 1,456 bytes and writes that into the host's least used units. Copying weights between its independently trained networks fails 0 of 12.

[I] So the packet route needs no correspondence between the two networks. That correspondence is the obstacle weft issue 149 called the hidden boss. The route goes around it.

[F] Three facts limit the claim today. A router that sits outside the network and switches on the same input channel matches the splice, 12 of 12. The splice is told which skill it carries. And in the E15 benchmark and the E11 rerun, an arm with the same information and none of the mechanism matched the favoured arm.

[F] On a small transformer the picture is the same and less flattering. The splice that the thread record reports as passing 2 of 2 seeds passes 13 of 20 fresh ones. An outside router passes 20 of 20. Trained with the task token hidden, a graft fires on every context.

[F] One belief fell. The record says a skill that moves data between positions cannot be spliced until an attention graft exists. A packet of 16,576 weights in the first position-wise block does the reversal on 8 of 8 seeds with native skills intact. It rewrites the position codes that the frozen later attention reads. An attention graft of one head, built as the record proposed, works on 5 of 8 seeds at best.

[I] So the splice thread is not blocked where the record thought. What every result in it leans on is the context signal: a token or channel, handed in, that says which skill is wanted.

[C] The test that would decide it: a skill whose context is not an input channel, so no outside router can be written, on a network that was trained elsewhere. If the packet route works there and a router cannot be built, the thread has new ground. If it fails, E13 is a well-made instance of gated modules.

### Admission as a behaviour claim that one input can refute

[F] The calibration study shows what an admission rule can and cannot mean. A distance bound means nothing for a deep network at any useful radius. A check on fresh probes tracks real change closely, and fails against a proposer who has seen the probes.

[F] The out-of-sample test of the two-check rule agrees. It admitted 48 of 48 benign grafts and none of 144 from the old harm classes. It also admitted 48 of 48 grafts carrying a trigger that the probes never reach.

[I] Weft already holds the rare parts of a different design. It has a predicate that agrees across three languages on 20,992 inputs, a replay verifier for execution records, and metering.

[I] With those, an update can be published as a claim: these two weight hashes, this input domain, this tolerance. Anyone who finds one input where the outputs differ by more than the tolerance refutes it. Checking the refutation costs two forward passes.

[K] Fraud proofs for machine-learning inference exist (opML, 2024). The search found none for a bound on the change between two versions of a self-modifying model.

[C] The test that would decide it: build the claim and the dispute check on the E13 harness, and run it against the trigger grafts. A claim over the input distribution will not catch a trigger of negligible mass, by Proposition 2 of the calibration theory. A claim over a stated input box will, if a search over inputs can find the trigger. Whether that search is strong enough is the open question.

## One subspace, two readings

[F] Issue 149 and the plasmid records E11, E12 and E13-transitive edit inside the null space of a set of keep inputs, and call that safe. The E15 benchmark and pull request 181 treat the same subspace as the place an attack hides.

[F] Measured on the 12 E13 hosts of the calibration study (exploratory): an edit inside the null space of 256 keep inputs still moves held-out inputs by 0.188 of what a random edit of the same length does. With 16 keep inputs the share is 0.551, and with 512 it is 0.124. Each doubling of the keep set cuts the leak by a fifth to a third, and every keep input uses up 8 silent directions. At 1,561 inputs none are left.

[I] So the null-space gate is a trade. It buys a factor of two to eight in quietness over a random edit, and pays for it in room to edit. It never buys silence on inputs it did not see. A proposer who knows the keep set can use that same leak: the adversary in the calibration study that stays inside the null space breaks held-out behaviour while the probes show almost nothing.

[F] The benign graft is a different kind of move. It travels about 28 units, where one optimiser step travels 1, and it disturbs native behaviour less than that one step does. Its safety comes from where it writes and what switches it on, not from any distance or projection.

[I] The reading that fits all six records: a move is safe on the inputs that cannot see it. Distance does not say which inputs those are. The keep set says it only for itself. Structure, such as unused units behind a switch, says it by construction, and is the only one of the three that could be certified for inputs nobody tested.

## Other convergences

- [F] **Machinery that never acts.** In the Plasm cycle the first lineage wins 120 of 120 contests. In the colony, 65 of 70 heirs change nothing. In the admission rule, the drift check never rejected a graft the agreement check had not already rejected. In the E11 rerun, the Frobenius arm cannot differ from the raw arm as coded. A cheap habit follows: report how often each mechanism changed an outcome.
- [F] **The gate is exactly right for a linear cell.** For the Plasm cell, which is one matrix, the Frobenius bound is a tight behaviour bound, and the radius for a chosen tolerance is one line of arithmetic. The same algebra that shows the bound is empty for a deep network gives weft issue 74 its radius rule.
- [F] **Two checkers are independent, the rest re-read.** The min-cut verifier rebuilt its network and rejected every tampered certificate. The Farkas verifier recomputes both sides. `verify_e15.py` accepted an edited and resealed result. The replay verifier now on weft main is the piece that could turn asserted provenance in the morphogenesis records into something measured.

## Threads to pull next, in order of information per unit of effort

1. **Scrambled-price control, then the second model family.** The control separates reading prices from following names and needs 4 to 7 runs per arm. A sense effect of 0.10 needs about 64 runs per arm with the fibmod task and 26 without. Needs approval for a small live spend.
2. **Fresh-probe admission.** Draw the probes after the candidate is fixed, state the bound of Proposition 2, and rerun the graft set. Pass: every graft built with sight of the old probes is caught. Then the dispute check of the second candidate above.
3. **Splice without an input switch.** The test named above. An attention graft is not the next step: a first-layer packet already does the reversal. Every splice record from now on carries the outside-router arm.
4. **Gate housekeeping with real effect.** Fix the float32 path. Adopt the radius rule for the linear cell. Settle the one open choice in the reject paths, where pull requests 211 and 173 disagree on what a rejected call returns.
5. **E15 with a fair harness.** Uniform scoring, teacher rows at random positions, inputs taken from data, and the plain-threshold arm as a standing baseline.
6. **Morphogenesis control.** Make the script run, then run the scrambled-graph arm at a matched budget.
7. **Ledger in CI.** Move `claims/check_claims.py` to `scripts/`, add it to the workflow, and give the claims register an expiry rule as issue 9 proposes.
8. **A proof about the real gate.** State and prove what an accept guarantees in floating point. That closes the gap between the Lean files and the code.

## Limits of this evaluation

- The literature search was short: four queries and two abstracts. "Not found" means not found in that search.
- The reruns of open pull requests were made by agent workers. The lead re-checked the items listed in the record README.
- Toy scale throughout. Nothing here was run on a pretrained model.
- The leak numbers in the convergence section are exploratory. They were not preregistered.
